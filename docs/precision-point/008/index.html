PRECISION POINT · No. 008

More padding. More pressure?

11 of 18

horses had lower peak force at the trot with the best pads (gel and fur) under one too-wide dressage saddle, measured on a pressure mat (The Veterinary Journal, 2010).

6 of 18

had more force with the leather pad — a trend that did not hold up statistically once horse-to-horse differences were accounted for. Same pad, different horse, opposite answer.

In a second study, under saddles that five qualified fitters had judged correctly fitted, a gel half pad gave the highest pressure at the front of the saddle in sitting trot and canter (Journal of Equine Veterinary Science, 2021).

Wrong saddle + more padding = a different problem. Not no problem. After your next ride, lift the saddle, look at the sweat pattern, and photograph any dry patches inside the wet. Show the photos to your saddle fitter.
