PRECISION POINT · No. 011

Three times your weight. Twice a stride.

~3×

the rider’s body weight: the two force peaks on the horse’s back in every sitting-trot stride (2.9× and 3.0×), in a motion-capture study of 13 riders who rode both sitting and rising trot. Average force was the same either way, equal to body weight (Journal of Biomechanics, 2010).

2.5× · 2.0×

the same two peaks in rising (posting) trot: the second peak about a third lower. For a 150-lb rider, sitting-trot peaks come to roughly 440–455 lb, about an upright piano, twice a stride (our arithmetic).

Next flat school:
Have someone film 20 strides of sitting trot and 20 of posting from the side, phone at saddle height, same direction, same arena. Write down how many minutes of each you rode.
Watch the clips side by side: the ears, the tail, and whether the back swings. Log what you see; read it with your trainer.

More sitting + longer sessions = more peak load on the same back. Sitting trot isn’t wrong. It’s a dose.
