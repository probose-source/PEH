PRECISION POINT · No. 013

Carrot stretches aren’t a trick.

8 horses · 3 months

Baited stretches (chin to chest, chin to girth, chin toward the hip, plus one up and out), 5 repetitions each, 5 days a week, with no riding (Equine Veterinary Journal, 2011).

6 of 6 levels grew

On ultrasound, the deep muscle along the spine (multifidus) grew at every level scanned, from mid-back to the loin, on both sides, and the left-right difference shrank. A second, independent study with a control group (Animals, 2025; 14 sedentary horses, 3 times a week for 8 weeks) found trot symmetry better than baseline from week 4.

This week at the barn:
Same stretches, both sides, same days, and the count written on the stall card.
One side photo of the back every Monday, same spot, whole back in the frame.
Ask your trainer: which stretches, how many times a week, and who’s counting?

Counted stretches + a calendar = more back muscle. The carrot is the bait; the count is the workout.
