PRECISION POINT · No. 007

Your horse’s heels want the ground.

Heel angle 30° → 35°

23 Thoroughbreds in full race training had their shoes pulled for 90 days. Their heel angle rose by almost five degrees while the toe angle held at 51°. The 13 that stayed shod, on the same farrier and the same 30-day trim: 28° to 28°.

The front of the hoof held; the back stood up. To follow your own horse, take a side photo of each front foot from the same spot at every trim, and watch the gap between the toe angle and the heel angle. The limits matter: one training yard, young racehorses with no turnout, a small shod group. The authors say plainly that barefoot is not for every horse: if wear outruns growth, shoes go back on. Pulling shoes is a call for you and your farrier.
